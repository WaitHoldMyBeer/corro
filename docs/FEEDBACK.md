# Lawyer feedback sheet (hold this while showing the live app)

About five minutes of walking, then the questions. Ask for honesty: "I want to hear what is wrong with it." Do not defend. Write what they say, in their words, in the table at the end. Do not read case details aloud beyond what is on screen, and do not leave the app with them.

## Walk-through (five minutes)

| Min | Screen | Click | Say (one sentence) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Case, as it opens | Nothing, let them read for 10 seconds, then ask "what do you see?" | "This is one matter read live from Clio, read-only." |
| 0:30 | River | Point at the gate, then click it | "Here is what the case is worth, what coverage can pay, and what is held back and why." |
| 1:15 | Notes say / document shows | Open one card, click the document side | "The notes say one thing and a page in the file shows another; we show both and do not pick." |
| 2:00 | Move, then Write | Click the top move, then "draft the message" | "One click opens a draft for the provider's office." |
| 2:45 | Write | Type one sentence the file contradicts; then one that discloses a dollar figure | "The checker underlines what the file contradicts and locks what a provider must not see." |
| 3:45 | Share | Toggle one category; point at the preview | "You choose, per provider, what leaves the firm, and you see exactly what they will see." |
| 4:30 | Provider page | Open the provider link in a second window | "This is what the provider sees: is the case alive, a coverage band, what you need from them." |

If anything breaks, say "that's a bug" and move on; do not apologise at length. Note it.

## Six questions

Ask in this order. Ask "why?" once after each answer and stop. Prefer concrete examples over opinions.

1. **First look.** "Without my explaining, what did you look for first on the first screen, and did you find it? What did you think the big numbers were?" (Listen for what they read first and what they misread. Note any number they asked about.)
2. **Trust.** "Which number or statement on screen would you not trust, and what would you do to check it before relying on it?" (Listen for sources, dates, who entered it. Ask which one they would verify in the file first.)
3. **The line on providers.** "A provider on a lien could see up to these six things. For each, would you share it, never, or it depends on the case?" Read them one at a time: status of the matter; coverage as a band (confirmed, being confirmed, none established); the provider's own bills; the provider's own records; requests the firm makes of their office; dates the patient attended their appointments. Then: "What would you want a provider to see that is not on this list? What on this list worries you?" (Record yes, no or depends for each. Note the reason on any no, and what is missing.)
4. **The live checker.** "If this checked your own draft, or your notes of a call with a provider's office, as you typed, would you leave it switched on? What would a false alarm cost you, and what would a missed contradiction cost you?" (Listen for tolerance: how many false alarms per page before they turn it off. Do not suggest a number.)
5. **Before it goes to a real provider.** "What would have to be true before you sent that provider link to a real provider's office?" (Listen for authentication, client consent, expiry, an audit trail, who sees it, wording, a human review. Record each condition.)
6. **Cut.** "If you could keep only part of this, and had to cut one thing, what would you cut?" (Do not ask what they would add. One answer, then ask why.)

## After

- Share the filled table with the team promptly after the last session; mark the two most repeated points.
- Sort: anything two or more lawyers said goes to the top. A statement one lawyer made once is a lead, not a finding.
- Do not report what lawyers "would pay" or "would use" as a number; this is five minutes with a few people, not a sample. Quote them instead.
